# Crystal Corridor

The flagship landing level: a neon crystal corridor that now shoots back, polished through many human playtests. It moves from a readable corridor warm-up through lancers and homing shard bolts into the Crystal Warden finale.

126 BPM, three acts, minor harmony, transport-quantized player sounds, a hidden kill-melody lane, and act-specific lock, fire, hit, and kill timbres.

A 45-second run with a 3-point hull, node, drifter, orbiter, and lancer waves, interceptable homing bolts, and a staged Crystal Warden boss.

# Rush

Rush is a night flight down a city street canyon built to test how fast this engine can feel. The speed rig is still the point, but the velocity now reads through asphalt lane dashes, tower faces, streetlights, gantries, traffic, FOV pulls, and the shared depth-reprojection blur.

## Visual language
Near-black low-poly city geometry with flat-shaded road, curbs, sidewalks, and skyscraper walls. Small cyan windows, cyan-white headlights, amber streetlights, taillights, and beat-strobing gantries provide the hot accents without turning bloom into a whiteout.

## Musical language
170 BPM and deliberately blunt: a relentless kick, closed hat, and one distorted bass figure. Player actions answer with terse zaps, ticks, and rejects inside the same harsh electronic palette.

## Mechanical signature
A 30-second speed-feel testbed with a long rail, authored speed surges, simple pods, strafing darts, and heavier armored targets. Scenery cars are non-interactive speed cues: same-direction traffic gets overtaken fast, while oncoming headlights close at absurd speed.

## What to read
- `src/levels/rush/tuning.ts`
- `src/levels/rush/gameplay.ts`
- `src/levels/rush/visuals/index.ts`
- `src/levels/rush/post-fx.ts`
- `src/levels/rush/audio.ts`

## Status & notes
Technical test fixture demonstrating high-speed engine features (e.g., rail pacing); however, as a stripped-down, limited testbed, it is excluded from the level picker and must not be used as a reference for level design quality or distinctiveness.

# Skyhook

A one-minute ascent in a white utility climber car. Skyhook begins in storm-grey weather, punches through a sunlit cloud deck, loses its blue in the stratosphere, and reaches a black sky where the planet curves below. The visual signature is practical elevator hardware edged in hazard orange; the musical signature is the air thinning until only the tether heartbeat remains.

## Visual language

Storm banks and cloud puffs sit behind a winding central tether, guide rails, service collars, and falling panel debris. Gusts and skiffs are broad, wind-riding silhouettes; carclaws visibly clamp onto the elevator; needles and voidlings become smaller, harder vacuum machines. Stars fade in above the indigo edge, a wireframe planet rolls below, and the station's white docking mouth opens for the final coda. Every enemy is built from low-poly panels, seams, hooks, and thin linework, with orange reserved for danger and hot cores.

## Musical language

120 BPM, 30 bars, and a D-minor-to-open-air ascent. The first five bars leave room around wide pad chords and wind noise. The cloud deck adds kick, bass, and short plucks; stratosphere and edge remove those layers bar by bar. The boss at bar 22 restores a low tether pulse and a clipped industrial bell. Lock, fire, hit, kill, volley, damage, and rejected-release sounds are all pitched from the live score; the final four bars decelerate into a sparse docking chord.

## Mechanical signature

The 60-second run has four hull points and 100 counted targets. Gust, skiff, and voidling waves move by sweep, bank, orbit, or plunge rather than hovering in place. Carclaws descend along the tether and damage the climber, while needles launch interceptable homing bolts. At bar 22 the staged Skyhook machine appears high above the car: three two-hit stages, a readable approach, an exposed tether core, and a lethal final reach if it is ignored. Severing it clears bars 26–30 for station docking.

## Status & notes

Benchmark showcase level. Target contract is a complete 55–65 second playable run; Skyhook is authored to 60 seconds. The gallery card is generated from this file; keep the identity `skyhook-v01u` and title `Skyhook` unchanged when tuning the level.
