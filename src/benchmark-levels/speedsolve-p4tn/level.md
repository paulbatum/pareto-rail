# Speedsolve

Sixty seconds, one boss, no corridor: a colossal twisting puzzle cube hangs in a pale, softly lit hall while the rail corkscrews around it and the world rolls a full turn. Four squares of the presented face arrive wrong and glow; destroy any of them and the cube snaps a quarter turn of that layer exactly on the next beat, one move closer to a single colour. Order never matters. Six faces reach one colour and blow their nine caps off in showers of loose cubies, six weakpoints rise out of the machinery underneath, and then the whole shell blooms open into a ring around a naked core.

## Visual language
The cube owns colour and nothing else may touch it: six candy solve hues on 54 proud caps over 26 white machined cubie bodies, against a pale grey-blue void with a receding floor grid, distant scaffold towers, drifting cubie flecks, and two colossal line-drawn gantry hoops that swing bodily around the puzzle as the camera rolls. Machinery is white, grey and graphite; the player's reticle, lock brackets and tracers are graphite with white-hot cores, so a shot never reads as a piece of the toy. Enemy fire borrows the cube's own colours because the little polyhedra are pieces of the same toy. The reticle is a six-pip solve counter. START and REPLAY are `SOLVE!` and `RESET!`, spelled in 5×7 grids of candy cubies pressed into dark machined tiles with pale bezels, one tile colour per letter — legible by contrast alone, with the bloom slider at zero.

## Musical language
144 BPM, 36 bars, exactly 60.000 seconds, in a five-bar D-dorian loop so every face section begins on the same harmony. The cube is the percussion section: one `snap` voice is both the track's backbeat and the sound of a layer landing, and because rotations are scheduled onto the next quarter note a snap the player causes is indistinguishable from one the arrangement was going to play. Above it the player is the soloist — locks climb the live chord, volleys fire a pitched release, and every kill plays the written note of a hidden two-bar melodic lane, so a chained volley walks a real melody. The arrangement adds a layer per face section, and each face actually conquered buys an extra glass counter-melody, so the mix thickens with the solve rather than just with the clock. The core section drops a sub drone under everything; the killing blow ducks the machine into a D-major bloom over a scatter of plastic clicks.

## Mechanical signature
A three-point hull and a metronomic, constant-speed rail. The cube arrives out of the hall showing its top face, then six five-bar sections: a three-quarter-bar swing rolls the world sixty degrees, quarter-turns the cube, and riffles the incoming face into its scramble; then four glowing wrong squares, one beat-locked layer snap per square destroyed, the face blowing off its nine caps, and a two-stage weakpoint piston rising out of the socket. The mechanism refuses a shot mid-snap: a release during a rotation has its cube targets denied while everything else still fires, and the HUD names the rule the first time it happens. Meanwhile ring-orbiting octahedra with interceptable bolts, corner-diving tetrahedra and full-width crossing prisms keep the reticle sweeping. Clearing a whole face in one release pays a 900-point solve bonus. At bar 32 the cubie shell expands into a ring — flattened away from the view axis so the two-stage core is never behind a block — and the core bursts into a confetti storm of tiny cubes.

## Status & notes
Built to the standing brief from the Speedsolve theme assignment. Inspection markers: `cubeArrival` (bar 0), `firstFace` (bar 2), `thirdFace` (bar 12), `fifthFace` (bar 22), `lastFace` (bar 27), `coreExposed` (bar 32 beat 3). Verified headless: typecheck, build, benchmark scope, audio trace, and the floor gate (simulation across all policies, target occlusion, performance). WSL2 cannot render WebGPU, so the real frame and the mix still need a human playtest. Check first that a layer snap reads as landing on the beat, that the four wrong squares stay legible against the white cubie bodies with bloom at zero, that the sixty-degree roll at each swing reads as swinging around the cube rather than as the camera tipping over, and that the caps blowing off a conquered face lands as a payoff — the conquest and confetti moments are the two things no headless capture could show.

One engine note for readers of `index.ts`: the shared post config forwards `bloom.threshold` and `bloom.radius` to three's `bloom(node, strength, radius, threshold)` in that order, so in this level's config the `threshold` field is the blur radius and the `radius` field is the luminance cut-off. The values are chosen so that only genuinely HDR cores and hot lines glow — a pale hall cannot survive a low cut-off.
