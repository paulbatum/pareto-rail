# Red Squall

The whole level is a duel with one pilot.

You are inside a storm, not above it. The world is a canyon of cloud: towering walls of grey vapour, rain streaking past the camera, and lightning going off deep inside the cloud so that whole cliff faces of it light up for a fraction of a second. Between strikes the world is nearly monochrome — graphite, slate, rain-white. There is exactly one hot colour in this level and it belongs to your rival: a crimson engine trail you learn to find in the murk.

The rival is with you almost the whole way. It comes head-on out of the rain, crosses your line close enough to feel, breaks off, vanishes into a cloud wall, sends its flight in on you while it recovers, and comes back. It keeps coming back, and every return has to show the cost: a torn wing, a fire it can't put out, a new roughness in the way it flies. Give its flying a temperament that changes — early on it is showing off, wasting moves it doesn't need; by the end it is flying like something with everything to lose. The player should understand all of this without a word of it being said. The ordinary enemies are its wingmen: they wear its colours, and none of them have its nerve.

Give the rival a tune. One motif on a lead voice with an edge on it, thrown at the player whenever the rival attacks — and the player's own kills answer it in the gaps, a duel inside the music as well as outside it. Under that, low strings and storm percussion with real thunder folded into the kit. When the rival is gone, the level goes genuinely quiet: rain, wind, and that motif faint and far away in the cloud, so the player is listening for it before they can see anything.

Finale: it takes you up through the roof of the storm into flat moonlight over an unbroken floor of cloud, and there is nowhere left for either of you to hide. Clean air, hard light, no cover, the motif finally played out in full. Kill it there. It goes down through the cloud floor, the storm closes over it, and the level ends in the quiet.
