# Grand Waltz

Build a level in three.

The world is a ballroom the size of a stadium, turning slowly in space — clockwork, gilt, and glass, tier on tier of balconies rising out of sight, chandeliers hanging in ranks down its length, a floor of dancers far below. Fill the frame. This should be the most crowded, most layered world in the set: things passing close to the camera, things moving far behind them, gold catching light at every depth.

The dancers are porcelain automata, and the ones that come for you are the same porcelain — white glaze, gilt seams, painted cheeks, visible joints. Nothing here glows. Light it warm: candle gold, deep burgundy, black lacquer, and the cold white of the porcelain. A broken automaton should shatter like a dropped cup.

Everything is in triple time. The music is a waltz — bowed-string weight, celesta and glass-bell figures above it, a real melody you could hum on the way out, gaining voices as the dance escalates — and the choreography has to be a dance, not a wave list. Targets arrive in threes, in turns, in sweeping figures across the floor. The strong beat is the ONE, and a volley that lands on it should feel like the whole room turned with you. Do not let a downbeat pass as filler.

The run should feel elegant and slightly wrong: a beautiful room full of things that are not alive, dancing perfectly, forever.

Boss: the Conductor, on the podium at the head of the room — an automaton twice the size of the rest, with too many arms. It conducts the ballroom at you: a baton phrase telegraphs on one bar and lands on the next, chandeliers drop on its cues, the dancers move when it tells them to. Take its arms one at a time, and let the music lose something each time an arm stops. When the last one falls the waltz stops with it, the ballroom is left turning in silence, and the level ends on a single held chord.
